Documents to Check Before Buying Property in Gujarat: A Practical Checklist

October 23, 2026 by Aaditya Properties Editorial Desk

In brief

Before paying any token amount in Gujarat, check the land record (7/12 and 8A, or the property card in city survey areas), the chain of title deeds, an encumbrance search on GARVI, the NA order for non-agricultural use, the approved plan and BU permission for buildings, and GujRERA registration for new projects. Most records can be viewed online on official portals, but a clean online record is not proof of clear title — have an advocate do a title search.

Buying property in Gujarat means checking two things: who owns it and whether it can legally be used the way you intend. The documents below answer those questions. For each one, this guide explains what it proves, where to find it, and what should make you stop and ask questions.

Land records: 7/12, 8A and mutation entries

In Gujarat’s rural and peri-urban areas, the 7/12 extract (village form 7/12) is the basic record for a survey number. It shows the holder’s name, the area, and rights or charges recorded against the land. The 8A extract lists all the holdings of a khata (account) holder. Mutation entries (VF-6) record each change, such as a sale, an inheritance or a bank charge.

You can view these free on AnyROR by district, taluka, village and survey number. Digitally signed copies and Revenue Department applications are handled on iORA.

Warning signs:

  • The seller’s name does not match the 7/12.
  • A mutation entry is still pending or was disputed.
  • A bank charge is recorded and has not been cleared.
  • There are several co-owners, but only one is selling.

Property card for city survey areas

For urban land surveyed by a City Survey Office, ownership is recorded on a property card rather than a 7/12. AnyROR has an urban section for viewing property cards. Check the same things: the owner’s name, the area, and any recorded charges.

Title deeds and the chain of ownership

The land record shows who is recorded as holding the land. The title deeds show how they got it. An advocate normally traces the chain of registered sale deeds and other transfer documents for about 30 years and matches each step against the mutation entries. This title search is professional work. A clean online record does not replace it.

Encumbrance search on GARVI

The Inspector General of Registration’s GARVI portal offers a “Search with EC Report” service. It lists registered documents for a property by sub-registrar office, survey or city survey number, and year range.

Limitation: it shows only registered documents. An unregistered agreement, a pending court case or an unpaid tax will not appear. That’s another reason to have an advocate do the title search.

NA order: can the land be used for a home?

Agricultural land in Gujarat needs non-agricultural (NA) permission before it can lawfully be used for housing or commercial building. For a plot or a building on former agricultural land, ask for the NA order and check that the permitted use matches yours. Applications and orders are handled through the Revenue Department, including online via iORA.

Approved plan and BU permission

For a built property, check that the building plan was approved by the local authority (municipal corporation, urban development authority or panchayat). Also check that Building Use (BU) permission was granted. BU permission is Gujarat’s equivalent of an occupancy certificate. Banks usually ask for the approved plan, the NA order and the BU permission before they lend.

Warning sign: construction that differs from the approved plan, such as extra floors or a changed use, or a building that has been occupied without BU permission.

GujRERA registration for new projects

Under the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016, projects above the size threshold must be registered with the state RERA before they are advertised or sold. The threshold is land over 500 sq m or more than eight apartments. Agents who sell in registered projects must also register.

On the GujRERA portal you can search registered projects and agents. A project’s page includes its approvals and quarterly progress updates. The RERA Act text is on India Code.

Warning signs:

  • No registration number in the advertising.
  • The project name or promoter differs from the portal.
  • Progress updates have stopped.

Dues, society NOC and the seller’s identity

  • Property tax: ask for the latest receipts from the local body, so unpaid tax doesn’t pass to you.
  • Society or association: for flats, get a no-dues certificate and an NOC for the transfer, if the society requires one.
  • Identity and authority: check every owner’s original ID. If someone is selling under a power of attorney, check that it is registered, still valid and covers the sale.

Common mistakes

  • Paying a token amount before seeing the 7/12 or property card and the title deeds.
  • Treating an online record as final proof of ownership.
  • Ignoring co-owners or legal heirs who are recorded but not signing.
  • Buying on former agricultural land without seeing the NA order.
  • Assuming a bank’s loan approval means the title is clean. Banks do their own checks, but those protect the bank.

What to do next

  1. Collect copies of every document above from the seller before you negotiate seriously.
  2. View the 7/12, 8A, mutation entries or property card yourself on AnyROR, and compare them with what the seller gave you.
  3. Run the GARVI encumbrance search for the period your advocate advises.
  4. For new projects, check the GujRERA registration and the latest quarterly update.
  5. Have an advocate do a full title search before you sign an agreement or pay a significant amount.
