How to Buy Your First Home in Gujarat: A Step-by-Step Guide

October 23, 2026 by Aaditya Properties Editorial Desk

In brief

Fix a budget that covers stamp duty, registration and other costs on top of the price. Check how much a bank can lend under RBI's loan-to-value limits. Verify the property's documents and, for new projects, its GujRERA registration before paying a token. Sign a written agreement, deduct 1% TDS if the price is ₹50 lakh or more, and register the sale deed at the sub-registrar office.

This guide walks through a first home purchase in Gujarat in the order things usually happen. Each step links to the official source, so you can confirm current rules.

Step 1: Work out the full budget, not just the price

The amount you need is the price plus the costs of buying:

  • Stamp duty and registration fee. In Gujarat these are paid on the higher of the agreement value and the government’s jantri (ready reckoner) value. Widely published rates are 4.9% stamp duty and 1% registration fee, with the registration fee waived for women who buy as sole owners. We could not locate the government notification for the women’s concession. Confirm the current figures with the official calculator on GARVI before you budget.
  • GST on under-construction property (not on ready, completed property). Ask the builder for the exact amount in writing.
  • Other costs: legal fees for the title search, loan processing fees, society transfer charges or deposits, maintenance deposits, and brokerage if an agent is involved.

Step 2: Check how much you can borrow

RBI caps how much of a home’s value a lender can finance (the loan-to-value, or LTV, ratio):

Loan amount Maximum LTV
Up to ₹30 lakh 90%
Above ₹30 lakh, up to ₹75 lakh 80%
Above ₹75 lakh 75%

The rest is your down payment. For homes above ₹10 lakh, stamp duty and registration are not included in the property cost used for this calculation, so you normally pay them from your own funds. Source: RBI Master Circular – Housing Finance (1 April 2025).

Rates are rising. RBI raised the policy repo rate by 0.25 percentage points to 5.50% on 7 October 2026 (RBI press release). Floating home-loan rates linked to the repo rate usually follow. When comparing offers, look at the spread over the benchmark, the processing fee, and how resets work.

Prepayment: under RBI’s Pre-payment Charges Directions, 2025, lenders may not charge a prepayment penalty on floating-rate loans to individuals for non-business purposes. This applies to loans sanctioned or renewed from 1 January 2026 (RBI notification).

Step 3: Shortlist and verify the property

Before paying any token amount:

  • New project: search for it on the GujRERA portal. Check that the registration number, promoter and project details match the advertising, and read the latest quarterly progress update.
  • Any property: check the land record (7/12 and 8A, or the property card), the chain of title, an encumbrance search, the NA order and BU permission. See our document checklist.
  • Visit more than once, at different times of day. Check water supply, drainage, access roads and parking.

Step 4: Pay a limited advance and get a written agreement

For projects covered by RERA, a promoter may not take more than 10% of the cost as an advance before a written agreement for sale is registered (section 13 of the RERA Act, 2016). The agreement should state:

  • the price
  • the payment schedule
  • the carpet area (the RERA definition)
  • the possession date
  • what happens if possession is delayed

Step 5: Arrange the loan

Apply with the bank you chose. The lender will do its own legal and technical checks and ask for documents such as the title papers, NA order, approved plan and BU permission. Read the sanction letter for the rate type, the spread, fees, and how EMI or tenure changes when rates reset.

Step 6: Deduct TDS if the price is ₹50 lakh or more

If the consideration or the stamp duty value is ₹50 lakh or more, the buyer must deduct 1% tax at source from the payment to the seller and deposit it with the government.

From 1 April 2026 this falls under section 393 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (formerly section 194-IA of the 1961 Act). It is filed using Form 141, which replaced Form 26QB. See the Income Tax Department’s guidance on section 393 and Form 141. Different rules apply if the seller is a non-resident; take professional advice.

Step 7: Pay stamp duty and register the sale deed

Buy e-stamp paper or pay stamp duty through the channels listed on GARVI. Book a sub-registrar appointment. Buyer, seller and two witnesses attend with original ID to execute and register the sale deed. Keep the registered deed and its index copy safely.

Step 8: After registration

  • Apply to update the land record (mutation) or property card, and the property tax record, in your name.
  • Transfer the society membership, and the electricity, water and gas connections.
  • Keep every receipt. You’ll need them for tax purposes and if you sell later.

Common first-time mistakes

  • Budgeting only for the price and the down payment, and forgetting stamp duty, registration and GST.
  • Paying a token before checking documents and RERA registration.
  • Relying on the bank’s approval as proof of clean title.
  • Forgetting to deduct and deposit TDS on purchases of ₹50 lakh or more.
